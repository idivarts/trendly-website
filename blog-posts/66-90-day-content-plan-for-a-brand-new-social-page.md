# Starting From Zero: A 90-Day Content Plan for a Brand-New Social Page

Every piece of social media advice assumes you already have something to work with — an audience to analyze, a "best time to post" pulled from your own data, a handful of viral posts to repeat. None of that exists on day one. You have an empty grid, zero followers, and no idea if anyone is even going to see what you post.

Planning for a brand-new page isn't a smaller version of planning for an established one. The goals are different, the metrics that matter are different, and the biggest risk isn't posting the wrong thing — it's quitting before you've given the account enough time to tell you anything. Here's a 90-day plan built for exactly that starting point.

## Why the first 90 days need a different plan

With an established account, you're optimizing: which pillar converts best, which format gets saves, when your audience is online. With a brand-new account, you don't have enough data to optimize anything yet. Trying to A/B test your way to growth in week two is a waste of energy — you'll be chasing noise.

The first 90 days have one job: generate enough consistent signal that by day 91, you actually have something to analyze. That reframes success. It's not "did this post go viral," it's "did I show up enough times, with enough range, that I now know what's worth doing more of."

Split the 90 days into three phases with different goals — establish, build the habit, then start listening.

## Days 1–30: Establish signal, not growth

Resist the urge to be everywhere. Pick **one or two platforms** where your actual customers already spend time, not the platform that's trending in your industry newsletter. A B2B tool founder posting daily on LinkedIn will out-perform the same founder spread thin across five platforms with nothing to show data on any of them.

Before the first post, write down two things:

- **3–5 content pillars.** The recurring themes you'll post about — product education, behind-the-scenes, customer problems you solve, industry opinion, proof of work. Without pillars, week three turns into "what do I post today?" panic.
- **A one-page brand voice note.** Three adjectives for how you sound, three words you'd never use, and one example post in your voice. This matters more with no followers than with 10,000 — early posts are the only signal anyone (including an AI model helping you draft) has to learn your tone from.

Post consistently for 30 days — even a modest 3–4x/week cadence — and resist checking follower counts daily. At this stage, followers are a lagging indicator of almost nothing.

## Days 31–60: Build the habit before you build the numbers

By month two, the goal shifts slightly: prove to yourself that this is sustainable, not just possible. A content plan that only works when you have a free Sunday afternoon will collapse the first busy week.

This is the phase to **batch**. Sit down once a week (or once every two weeks) and draft 5–10 posts across your pillars in one sitting, rather than starting from a blank page every morning. It's also the right time to introduce a second format if you've only been doing one — a short video alongside static posts, or a carousel alongside single images — so you have enough range to see what your audience responds to once there is an audience.

Don't expect virality. Expect *consistency* to compound: the fifteenth post in a pillar almost always performs better than the first, because you've gotten sharper at the hook, the pacing, and the specific words your audience reacts to.

## Days 61–90: Start listening and adjusting

Somewhere around day 45–60, you'll have your first real signal — a post that got more saves, a format that got more comments, a pillar that consistently underperforms. This is the first point where looking at your own numbers is actually more useful than looking at general "best practice" advice.

Three things to check at day 90:

- **Which pillar earns the most engagement relative to how often you post it** — not just which post has the highest raw number.
- **Which format (video, carousel, single image, text) your specific audience actually stops scrolling for.** This varies enormously by niche; there's no universal answer.
- **What people are actually saying in comments and DMs.** Early on, this is a far richer data source than analytics dashboards with small sample sizes — read every reply.

Use what you learn to drop the pillar that isn't working and double down on the one that is, rather than restarting the whole strategy from scratch.

## What "no followers" actually buys you

It's easy to treat a small following as pure disadvantage. It isn't. A brand-new page has almost no reputational risk — nobody is comparing today's post to a hundred better ones you've already published. That's the best possible window to experiment: try the format you're unsure about, post the opinion you'd normally soften, test a voice that's a little bolder than your "safe" draft. The posts that feel like a stretch in month one are often the ones that define the account by month six.
