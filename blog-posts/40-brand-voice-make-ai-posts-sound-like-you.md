# Brand Voice: How to Make Every AI-Written Post Sound Like You

AI can write thirty captions in the time it takes you to reread this sentence. That used to be the hard part. It isn't anymore.

The new hard part is that most of those thirty captions sound the same — polished, upbeat, forgettable, and interchangeable with every other brand feeding a similar prompt into a similar model. When writing gets cheap, the thing that stands out isn't volume. It's voice.

Your brand voice is the one asset a language model can't generate for you out of thin air. It comes from who you are, who you serve, and how you talk to them. But you *can* teach it to the AI — and once you do, every post it drafts starts sounding like it came from your team instead of from nobody in particular.

Here's how to define that voice and put it to work.

## Why voice matters more now that AI writes the words

Think about the brands you actually remember on your feed. It's rarely because they posted more than everyone else. It's because they sound like a specific person with a specific point of view — dry and funny, or warm and reassuring, or blunt and technical. You could cover the logo and still know who was talking.

That recognizability is voice. And it's exactly what generic AI output erodes. A model with no guidance defaults to the average of everything it has ever read, which is smooth, safe, and completely anonymous.

So the goal isn't to stop using AI to write. It's to give the AI enough of *you* that it stops writing like the average and starts writing like your brand. A clear voice guide is what closes that gap.

## What "brand voice" actually is

Voice isn't a personality quiz or a mood board. It's a small, concrete set of rules about how your brand sounds in words. Three things make it up:

- **Tone** — the emotional register. Are you playful or serious? Formal or casual? Encouraging or challenging?
- **Vocabulary** — the words you reach for and the ones you avoid. A developer tool and a skincare brand describe "results" in very different language.
- **Rhythm** — sentence length and structure. Short and punchy? Long and considered? Lots of questions, or none?

Get those three right and your posts feel consistent even when the topic changes and even when a different person (or a different model) wrote the draft.

## Build your voice guide in an afternoon

You don't need a branding agency for this. You need a single page you can hand to a teammate — or paste into an AI prompt. Work through these five steps.

**1. Name the relationship.** Before tone, decide who you are *to your audience*. A knowledgeable friend? A no-nonsense coach? A behind-the-scenes guide? This one choice quietly settles a hundred smaller decisions about how you sound.

**2. Pick three adjectives — and one you're not.** Choose three words your voice should always be (say, "clear, warm, a little irreverent") and one it should never be ("stiff"). The contrast matters more than the adjectives themselves; "warm but never saccharine" tells you exactly where the line is.

**3. Write a short do / don't list.** Five of each. "Do use plain words. Don't use jargon unless our audience uses it first. Do start with the point. Don't open with 'In today's fast-paced world.'" These rules are what you'll actually enforce.

**4. Collect vocabulary.** List words and phrases that are on-brand, and a few that are off-limits. Every brand has words it would never say — write them down so nobody (human or AI) reaches for them.

**5. Show two examples.** One paragraph in your voice, one obviously not in your voice, labeled. Examples teach faster than rules. If you can only do one thing on this list, do this one.

That's the whole guide. A page, maybe less.

## Turn your voice guide into a prompt the AI can use

A voice guide sitting in a doc doesn't help your captions. The trick is to feed it to the model every time it writes.

The lazy version — "make it sound like our brand" — does nothing, because the model has no idea what that means. The version that works pastes the actual rules into the prompt:

> "Write three Instagram captions for [topic]. Voice: clear, warm, a little irreverent — never stiff or corporate. Short sentences. Plain words, no jargon. Start with the point. Never open with a rhetorical 'Ever wondered...' Here's an example of our voice: [paste your labeled example]."

The difference between those two prompts is the difference between anonymous filler and something you'd actually publish.

This is also where working across several AI models pays off. Different models have different default personalities — one leans punchy, another leans thorough, another writes cleaner short-form. When you're not locked into a single tool, you can point the same voice guide at whichever model best matches the tone you're after, then edit from there. Setting your voice once and reusing it everywhere is exactly the kind of thing a single workspace should handle for you, instead of you re-explaining your brand in every new chat window.

## Keep the voice consistent across a team

The moment more than one person posts, voice drift creeps in. A founder writes casually, a new hire writes formally, a freelancer writes in their own style, and within a month the feed sounds like three different companies.

The voice guide is your defense. Make it the shared reference everyone writes against — and, just as important, the thing everyone *reviews* against. When a draft comes through, the question isn't only "is this accurate?" but "does this sound like us?" A quick review-and-approve step, where a second set of eyes checks tone before anything publishes, catches drift before your audience ever sees it. That's true whether the first draft came from a person or a prompt.

## A two-minute voice self-audit

Pull up your last ten posts and read them out loud. Ask:

- Could I tell these came from *us* with the logo hidden?
- Do they sound like one voice, or several?
- Is there a sentence in here I'd never actually say to a customer?
- Where does it slip into generic marketing-speak?

If the answers are uncomfortable, that's not a failure — it's your voice guide writing itself. Every weak spot you find is a rule worth adding.

## Voice is the one thing AI can't copy for you

Here's the reassuring part. As AI makes the mechanics of writing effortless, the *editorial* decisions — what you stand for, how you talk, what you'd never say — become the real differentiator. Those are yours. The model can execute your voice beautifully once it knows what your voice is, but it can't invent it.

So spend the afternoon. Write the one page. Then let AI do the heavy lifting of drafting, while you do the light-but-decisive work of making sure it still sounds like you.

That's how you get the best of both: the speed of AI and the recognizability of a real brand.
