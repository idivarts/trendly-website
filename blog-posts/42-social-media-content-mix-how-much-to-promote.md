# How Much Should You Promote? Finding Your Social Media Content Mix

Two feeds fail for opposite reasons. One is wall-to-wall promotion — every post a discount, a launch, a "link in bio." People scroll past it because it only ever asks for something. The other never mentions the product at all — endless tips and memes that build a following but sell nothing, because no one ever learns what you do.

The difference between a feed that grows *and* converts isn't a cleverer caption or a better hook. It's the **mix** — the ratio of posts that give versus posts that ask. Get the ratio right and every promotional post lands softly, because you've earned the audience's attention with everything around it.

Here's how to find your mix, plan around it, and keep it balanced week after week.

## Why the mix matters more than any single post

Social media runs on a simple exchange: people give you their attention, and they expect something back. Usually that "something" is useful, entertaining, or relatable — rarely is it a sales pitch. When you promote, you're spending attention you've banked. Post too many pitches in a row and the account goes overdrawn: reach drops, unfollows tick up, and even your good posts get buried because the algorithm reads low engagement as "people don't want this."

So the goal isn't to promote *less* forever. It's to promote from a position of credit — to earn enough goodwill between pitches that the pitches feel welcome. That's what a content mix is: a deliberate balance between depositing attention and spending it.

## The four jobs your content does

Most feeds try to do everything at once and end up doing nothing clearly. It's easier to think of every post as doing one of four jobs:

- **Educate** — teach something useful. How-tos, tips, myths debunked, behind-the-decision explainers. This is your credibility engine.
- **Entertain** — make people smile, react, or share. Relatable takes, light behind-the-scenes, trends adapted to your world. This is your reach engine.
- **Connect** — build relationship and trust. Founder story, customer spotlights, values, replies, questions to the audience. This is your loyalty engine.
- **Promote** — ask for the sale or the sign-up. Product posts, launches, offers, testimonials that lead somewhere. This is your revenue engine.

The first three all *deposit* attention. Only the last one *spends* it. A healthy feed leans heavily on the first three so the fourth can do its job.

## A starting ratio (not a law)

You've probably heard the "80/20 rule" — roughly 80% value, 20% promotion. It's a useful rule of thumb, not a scientific law. Treat it as a starting line, not a finish line.

A practical split to begin with, over a typical week or ten posts:

- **~40% educate** — the bulk of your value.
- **~25% entertain** — keeps reach alive and the feed human.
- **~15% connect** — story, community, replies.
- **~20% promote** — clear, confident asks.

If you're brand new and nobody knows you yet, dial promotion down and connection up — people buy from accounts they feel they know. If you have an engaged audience and a launch coming, it's fine to raise promotion for a few weeks. The ratio is a dial, not a rule carved in stone. What matters is that you're choosing it on purpose instead of letting a sales-heavy panic week decide it for you.

## Map the mix onto your content pillars

The mix answers *what job* a post does; your [content pillars](/blog/content-pillars-picking-themes-your-brand-posts-about) answer *what it's about*. They work together. Take your three-to-five pillars and, for each one, note which jobs it usually serves. A "how it's made" pillar mostly educates and connects. A "customer results" pillar connects and promotes. A "team culture" pillar entertains and connects.

Now planning gets concrete: instead of staring at a blank week wondering what to post, you're filling slots — "this week I need three educate posts, one connect, and one promote" — and pulling each from the right pillar. The blank page disappears.

## Make the mix visible on your calendar

A mix you can't see is a mix you won't keep. The fastest way to stay honest is to look at your month laid out and *see* the balance.

Color-code your calendar by job — one color for educate, entertain, connect, promote. Then scan it. If half the tiles are promotion-colored, you'll spot it instantly, long before your audience feels pitched to. If there's a two-week stretch with no connection posts, that's a gap to fill. This is exactly what a visual content calendar is for: not just *when* you post, but whether the whole picture is balanced. ## Adjust with what the numbers tell you

The starting ratio is a hypothesis. Your analytics are the test. After a few weeks, look at which *jobs* actually perform — not just which posts. If your educate posts drive saves and your connect posts drive comments but your promotion posts quietly convert, that's your mix working as designed. If promotion is tanking reach, you're spending faster than you're depositing — ease off and rebuild goodwill.

## A simple weekly checkpoint

You don't need a spreadsheet. Once a week, ask three questions:

1. **Did I promote from credit?** Was there enough value around my asks that they felt earned?
2. **Is any bucket empty?** No connection posts in two weeks is a slow leak — patch it.
3. **Does next week's ratio fit what's coming?** Launch soon? Nudge promotion up. Quiet month? Lean into value.

Five minutes, and your mix stays intentional instead of drifting toward "post whatever's urgent."
